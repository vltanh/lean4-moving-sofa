# Schanuel excludes every algebraic direction at the model crossing

**Conditional analytic theorem draft.** Assume Schanuel's conjecture. Then no real solution of the contact and crossing-area equations in the specified 135..140-degree model box has algebraic exp(i beta). Consequently cos(beta), sin(beta), and tan(beta) are transcendental, and the direction is not straightedge-and-compass constructible. This strengthens the earlier conditional exclusion of rational multiples of pi. It is not an unconditional theorem, does not exclude elementary exp/log expressions by itself, and does not identify the model with the unrestricted global transition.

All algebraic closures below are taken inside C; Qbar denotes the algebraic numbers. Set

    b=beta, t=T, k=(1/2)sqrt(1+3/sin(b)^2), K=k(pi-b),
    z=exp(it), w=exp(2mu t), u=exp(iK).

The contact equation is P(z)w+Q(z)=0. `CONTACT_TRANSCENDENCE.md` proves that P,Q are coprime quadratics, their constant coefficients are nonzero, and w=-Q(z)/P(z).

## 1. The exact field form of the area relation

On the matching locus, the reduced area in `EXACT_CONTACT_CROSSING.md` is

    W=b/4+a t+C_b(z),     a=-(1-2d)/[2(1+2d)] != 0,

where d=cos b and C_b is a nonconstant rational function, with coefficients algebraic over exp(ib). Explicitly, for alpha=b/2-t,

    C_b(z)={-sin b+[(1-4d)cos alpha-(1+2d^2)]/(3sin alpha)}/(1+2d).

Here exp(i alpha)=exp(ib/2)/z. The reverse area is

    V(pi-b)=(pi-b)/(2+d)+D_b+E_b R_b(u),
    D_b=(1-2d)/(4sin b), E_b=3d^2/[2sin b(2+d)^2] != 0,
    R_b(u)=eta_R(u^2-1)/[i(u^2+1)+eta_R(u^2-1)].

As a rational function of u^2, R_b is nonconstant: its Mobius determinant is 2i eta_R != 0. The area equality therefore makes u algebraic over Qbar(pi,b,t,z) whenever exp(ib) is algebraic. If u is already algebraic, that equality instead solves linearly for t over Qbar(pi,b,z).

The linear coefficients will also be useful later:

    a t + A_b b + B_b pi + C_b(z)-D_b-E_b R_b(u)=0,
    A_b=1/4+1/(2+d), B_b=-1/(2+d), A_b+B_b=1/4.       (1)

## 2. Assume exp(ib) is algebraic

Then all the coefficients above, mu, and k are algebraic. Put

    V0=span_Q{pi,b},   r0=dim_Q V0 in {1,2}.

The field Qbar(pi,b) has transcendence degree at most r0: if r0=1 the two numbers are rationally proportional, and if r0=2 it has two generators. The two exponentials exp(i pi) and exp(ib) are algebraic.

First prove, using Schanuel and the CONTACT equation alone, that

    t is not in span_Qbar{pi,b}.                      (2)

Unconditionally z is transcendental by the Gelfond-Schneider argument in `CONTACT_TRANSCENDENCE.md`. Thus t cannot be in V0: rational linear combinations of pi,b have algebraic exponentials. Choose a Q-basis of i V0 and append it and 2mu t, where the appended first argument is i t. These r0+2 numbers are Q-linearly independent. Independence of the purely imaginary ones follows from t not in V0; the remaining number 2mu t is real and nonzero.

If t were in the algebraic linear span in (2), all arguments would belong to Qbar(pi,b). Their exponentials, apart from algebraic constants, would be z and w; w is rational in z. The resulting field would have transcendence degree at most r0+1, contradicting Schanuel's lower bound r0+2. This proves (2).

## 3. Add the reverse frequency: two exhaustive rank cases

Set

    V1=span_Q{pi,b,k(pi-b)},   r1=dim_Q V1.

Because k is algebraic, V1 is contained in span_Qbar{pi,b}, and r1 is r0 or r0+1.

### Case A: r1=r0+1

By (2), a Q-basis of i V1 together with i t and 2mu t has r0+3 independent arguments. Their inputs and exponentials generate a field algebraic over

    Qbar(pi,b,t,z),

since the contact equation determines w and the area equation makes u algebraic. Its transcendence degree is at most r0+2. This contradicts Schanuel.

A Q-basis need not consist of the original displayed arguments, but exponentials of its rational linear combinations are algebraic over the field generated by the original exponentials. Conversely the original arguments are rational linear combinations of the basis. This does not change the transcendence-degree bound.

### Case B: r1=r0

Now k(pi-b) is a rational linear combination of pi,b. Hence u is algebraic, because their exponentials are algebraic. Equation (1), with a nonzero, makes t algebraic over Qbar(pi,b,z). The field for a basis of i V0, i t, and 2mu t consequently has transcendence degree at most r0+1, but its r0+2 arguments are independent as above. Schanuel again gives a contradiction.

Both cases are impossible. Therefore exp(ib) is transcendental under Schanuel. No assumption that k or b/pi has a particular algebraic degree is needed, and no finite rational-denominator exclusion is used.

## Consequences and limitations

An algebraic sin b or cos b would make exp(ib) algebraic through a quadratic equation. A finite algebraic tan b would make exp(2ib) algebraic. Thus these coordinates are all transcendental under the same hypothesis. In particular all algebraic/constructible directions are excluded, not just rational angles.

This still allows elementary angles with transcendental coordinates, such as a nonzero algebraic angle in radians. Excluding finite exp/log expressions needs a separate tower argument; it does not follow from this theorem alone.

The proof uses Schanuel's conjecture in ranks at most five. It is an ordinary mathematical deduction requiring independent review, not a kernel-checked proof. Schanuel is not proved here.
