# Reduced exp/log towers and two contact lemmas

**Conditional mathematical lemmas.** Schanuel's conjecture is assumed throughout. These arguments concern exact constants, not elementary inversion as a function and not bounded numerical recognition. They are proof drafts requiring independent review. No proof of Schanuel or unconditional non-elementarity is asserted.

Let L be the smallest algebraically closed subfield of C containing Qbar and closed under exp and all complex logarithms of nonzero elements. This contains every finite expression from algebraic constants using field operations, algebraic roots, exponentials, logarithms, circular/hyperbolic functions, and their usual inverses. Allowing arbitrary algebraic roots makes L at least as large as the usual radical-based elementary-expression class. No new inverse of the sofa equations is admitted by definition.

## 1. Reduced towers over Qbar

A finite tower is a list a_1,...,a_n. Put

    F_0=Qbar,
    F_j=acl(F_{j-1}(a_j,exp(a_j))) inside C.

At each step either a_j belongs to F_{j-1} or exp(a_j) belongs to F_{j-1}. These cover an exponential step and a logarithmic step, respectively. Every finite set of elements of L is contained in the top field of such a tower. Algebraic operations are absorbed by the algebraic closures; an exponential or logarithmic operation adds one argument as needed.

The tower can be chosen reduced: a_1,...,a_n are Q-linearly independent. If a_j is a rational linear combination of earlier arguments, then a_j and exp(a_j) already belong to F_{j-1}. The exponential belongs because its integer power is a product of integer powers of the earlier exponentials and F_{j-1} is algebraically closed. Delete that redundant step, without changing any subsequent field, and repeat.

Under Schanuel a reduced tower has

    trdeg_Q F_j=j.                                    (1)

Each step adds at most one to the transcendence degree, while Schanuel applied to the first j arguments gives the matching lower bound. Therefore exactly one of a_j,exp(a_j) is transcendental over F_{j-1}; the other belongs to F_{j-1}.

This is the algebraically closed version of the reduced-tower method discussed by Chow. The definitions and proof are included here so that no stronger unquoted tower theorem is being assumed.

## 2. Saturation of a finite reduced tower

If F=F_n is such a tower and both x and exp(x) belong to F, then

    x in span_Q{a_1,...,a_n}.                         (2)

Otherwise the n+1 arguments a_1,...,a_n,x would be Q-linearly independent but their arguments and exponentials would lie in a field of transcendence degree n, contradicting Schanuel.

For any rational combination x=sum r_j a_j, its exponential is an algebraic monomial in exp(a_j). This description includes roots and any root-of-unity ambiguity, since all fields contain Qbar and are algebraically closed.

## 3. Capture lemma for two incommensurable exponential arguments

Let F=F_n, let c in F be nonrational, and let x be any complex number. Suppose

    exp(x), exp(c*x) are algebraic over F(x).          (3)

Then x belongs to F.

Indeed, the field generated by F, x, cx, and the two exponentials has transcendence degree at most n+1. If x,cx were Q-linearly independent modulo span_Q{a_j}, Schanuel would require n+2. Thus there are rational r,s, not both zero, with

    (r+s c)x in span_Q{a_j} subset F.

Since c is not rational, r+s c is nonzero. Division proves x in F. Algebraic closedness then places both exponentials in F as well.

This lemma does not assume x is elementary. It is precisely the step that will infer elementary switching data from a hypothetical elementary bend, using the area equation as well as the contact equation.

## 4. A rational contact graph contains no nonconstant monomial curve

This lemma itself is UNCONDITIONAL algebra. Let F be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, v transcendental over F, A,B in F nonzero, and r,s rational. Let P,Q in F[X] be coprime, with deg P>0 and P(0) nonzero. If

    P(A v^r) B v^s+Q(A v^r)=0,                       (4)

then r=s=0.

Choose an integer m clearing both rational denominators and put u=v^(1/m); u is still transcendental. The expression in (4) is a Laurent polynomial in u and must vanish identically. If mr is nonzero, choose a nonzero root zeta of P, and choose u_0 in F nonzero such that A u_0^(mr)=zeta. Such u_0 exists because F is algebraically closed. Substitution into the Laurent identity would give Q(zeta)=0, contrary to coprimality. Hence r=0. Now P(A) cannot vanish, since otherwise Q(A) would also have to vanish. Equation (4) forces u^(ms) to be constant, so s=0.

The coprimality and nonzero-root assumptions matter. A graph w=z^2 contains monomial curves and does not satisfy the hypothesis on the denominator P. Its solutions can certainly be elementary.

## 5. Conditional non-elementarity for a fixed algebraic contact equation

As an additional consequence, suppose c is algebraic irrational, P,Q have algebraic coefficients satisfying Section 4, and x is a solution of

    P(exp(x)) exp(c*x)+Q(exp(x))=0.                  (5)

Then x is not in L.

If x were in L, take a reduced tower of minimal length containing x,cx and their exponentials. By Section 2 both arguments are rational linear combinations of the tower arguments. Let F=F_{n-1} and a=a_n.

If a is transcendental over F (a logarithmic step), write x=x_0+r a and cx=y_0+s a with rational r,s and x_0,y_0 in F. Comparing coefficients of the transcendental a yields s=c r. Irrationality of c forces r=s=0. The arguments and their exponentials then lie in F, contradicting minimality.

If exp(a) is transcendental over F (an exponential step), a belongs to F, so both arguments lie in F. Their exponentials are A exp(a)^r and B exp(a)^s for A,B in F nonzero. Section 4 forces r=s=0. Again the arguments and their exponentials all lie in F, contradicting minimality.

The n=0 case is impossible as well: if x,cx and both exponentials were algebraic, Schanuel would require x=cx=0, whereas then (5) would contradict Section 4's hypotheses only if P(1)+Q(1) is nonzero. Therefore the conclusion must either assume x!=0 or explicitly exclude P(1)+Q(1)=0. In the application x=iT is nonzero. With x!=0, Schanuel on x alone already contradicts algebraicity of both x and exp(x).

Thus this result is stated with x!=0. It generalizes the earlier switching-data transcendence obstruction to exclusion of all finite algebraic exp/log expressions for fixed algebraic hallway direction. It does not by itself exclude an elementary hallway angle whose direction is transcendental.

## Source and scope

Timothy Y. Chow, *What is a closed-form number?*, American Mathematical Monthly 106 (1999), 440-448, Section 3, presents reduced towers and conditional constant-level non-elementarity: https://arxiv.org/html/math/9805045 . The algebraically closed tower, capture lemma, and rational-contact descent above are explicitly proved here as applications of that method. They are not attributed as stated results of Chow. Complex logarithms, including their branch constants, remain within L. The arguments require Schanuel, not just Lindemann-Weierstrass or Gelfond-Schneider.
