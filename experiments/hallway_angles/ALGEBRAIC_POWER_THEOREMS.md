# Why known algebraic-power independence theorems do not close the irrationality proof

This note records a literature check relevant to the hypothetical rational-bend case of the analytic contact-model crossing. It is not a claim about the unrestricted global beta_c.

Under beta/pi in Q, the sofa equations involve several transcendental quantities generated by algebraic exponents, including

    exp(mu q pi),
    exp(i kappa pi),

where mu and kappa are algebraic and kappa is irrational by `RATIONAL_BEND_FREQUENCY.md`.

It is tempting to invoke the deep Gel'fond-Chudnovsky-Nesterenko theory of algebraic powers. The available theorems are powerful but do not match the needed pair.

## Structured-power theorems

Classical results include:

- Gel'fond's theorem for cubic irrational gamma: alpha^gamma and alpha^(gamma^2) are algebraically independent.
- Later results of Chudnovsky, Brownawell-Waldschmidt, Diaz, Philippon, and Nesterenko give lower bounds for the transcendence degree among the structured family

      alpha^gamma, alpha^(gamma^2), ..., alpha^(gamma^(d-1))

  when gamma is algebraic of degree d.

Nesterenko's 1985 theorem, for example, guarantees at least floor(log_2(d+1)) algebraically independent numbers among that power sequence.

These are genuine unconditional algebraic-independence results, much stronger than individual Gelfond-Schneider transcendence.

## Mismatch with the sofa exponents

The sofa exponents mu and kappa satisfy

    kappa^2 = ((1-beta/pi)^2/4) * (4 mu^2+5).

This is a quadratic algebraic relation, but in general neither exponent is a rational power of the other, nor are they known to be consecutive powers gamma^j of one algebraic generator in a way that identifies the SPECIFIC two exponentials appearing in the sofa equations with the guaranteed independent subset.

A primitive-element representation of the number field does not fix this: the structured-power theorems say that some members of a much larger sequence are algebraically independent, not that arbitrary prescribed algebraic linear/polynomial combinations of the exponent are independent.

Moreover, the forward contact relation uses exp(2 mu T), where the base exp(iT) and T are themselves transcendental. Algebraic-power theorems require an algebraic base. Thus the most direct forward relation falls outside their hypotheses altogether.

## Modular special cases also do not apply

Nesterenko's modular-function theorem proves notable special algebraic independence statements such as pi and exp(pi sqrt(n)) for positive integers n. The hypothetical rational sofa bend cannot reduce kappa to sqrt(r) with rational r: if kappa were rational or had kappa^2 rational, then sin(beta)^2 would be rational, contradicting Niven's theorem in the 135--140 degree range.

More generally kappa lies in a real cyclotomic extension whose degree depends on the hypothetical rational denominator; it is not forced into the imaginary-quadratic/CM configurations where modular methods provide special evaluations.

## Conclusion

Known algebraic-power theorems materially strengthen the surrounding landscape but do not prove the required algebraic independence among the exact sofa quantities. The remaining gap is not individual transcendence and not merely a missing numerical estimate.

This does not prove that no classical theorem can be combined more cleverly with the sofa equations. It documents why the standard structured-power and modular special cases do not directly settle the rational-bend hypothesis.

Primary sources checked in the research session include Brownawell-Waldschmidt (Acta Arith. 32, 1977), Nesterenko (Math. USSR-Sb. 51, 1985), and Nesterenko's 1996 modular-function paper.
