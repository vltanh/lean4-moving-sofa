## 1. What the mathematical claim should be

Suggested wording, after review and verification of the refactor:

> We strengthen the maximizer reductions in Baek's argument so that they apply
> to every maximizing cap, rather than only to a selected balanced maximizer.
> Combined with Baek's fixed-angle existence theorem and quadratic upper bound,
> these reductions recover Gerver's optimality without using Baek's final
> optimality theorem as an input. The equality case identifies all right-angle
> maximizing caps, and a containment and regular-closedness argument recovers
> every optimal sofa as a set.

Use **a maximizer-first strengthening of Baek's proof** or **an alternative
assembly from Baek's intermediate results**. Do not use "independent of
Baek's proof", "a new solution from first principles", or "uniqueness for
free". The quadratic functional, its upper-bound property, and its maximum at
Gerver's triple are still central inputs. The equality analysis and recovery
are substantive additions.

Do not replace Baek's attribution: his theorem remains the original optimality
result. The new contribution is the extension to arbitrary maximizers and the
classification of equality cases, not priority for the area bound.

## 2. The dependency issue in the present manuscript

The existing `fact:optimal` in `sections/02-setting.tex` bundles three different
facts: Gerver's explicit area is greater than `11/5`; every moving sofa has area
at most Gerver's; and every cap functional has that upper bound. The second and
third conclusions cannot be inputs to an argument advertised as recovering
optimality.

`prop:reduction` in `sections/03-reduction.tex` currently uses that bound both to
show that the envelope of an equality-case sofa has the same area and to infer
that its cap is maximizing. `thm:caps` in `sections/08-equality.tex` also uses
it to equate maximality with having Gerver's area. Those are legitimate uses in
the old organization, but not before the bound is established in the new one.

Merely adding a remark saying "this also proves optimality" does not change
those dependencies. The integration must separate the primitive inputs from
the conclusions and move the equality-case reduction downstream.

## 3. Inputs to retain, with their roles separated

1. **Feasibility and explicit information about Gerver.** Its cap belongs to the
   right-angle cap class, its sofa has area `M > 11/5`, it has the canonical
   quadratic triple, and `Q(x_G) = M`. No universal upper bound is included.
2. **Fixed-angle existence and comparison.** For each `0 < omega <= pi/2`, a cap
   maximizing `A_omega` exists. One such cap is the cap of a monotone moving
   sofa; its sofa area dominates all moving sofas with that angle. This uses
   Baek's Theorems 3.5.2--3.5.6, not Theorem 1.1.1.
3. **The initial angle and monotonization facts.** A sofa of area at least `11/5`
   admits an angle in `[arcsec(11/5), pi/2]`, and its own monotonization is a
   containing moving sofa. Without a proved area bound, assert containment and
   nondecrease of area, not preservation of area.
4. **The quadratic comparison on the injectivity domain.** For `K` in that
   domain, `A(K) <= Q(x_K) <= Q(x_G) = M`, together with the Mamikon square-gap
   structure. This is a restricted-domain result from Baek's intermediate
   machinery, not his final global bound.

The existing construction of a balanced maximizer is still used to establish
existence and the comparison property. It is its special geometric
balancedness hypothesis that the new arbitrary-maximizer principles avoid.
Do not claim to eliminate the whole compactness/balanced-cap construction.

## 4. Mathematical spine of the revised proof

### A. Every right-angle maximizer has the required geometry

Let `K` actually maximize `A = A_(pi/2)` over all right-angle caps. Gerver's cap
is a competitor, so `A(K) >= M > 11/5`. In particular its area functional is
positive. The penalized selection and curvature arguments then prove the
injectivity condition for this specified `K`. Also `|K| >= A(K) >= M`, so the
cap satisfies the area threshold in the definition of the injectivity domain.

This step uses maximality itself. Do not start with `A(K) = M` and use global
optimality to obtain maximality.

### B. Determine the value and then the equality case

For such a maximizer,

```math
M \leq \mathcal A(K)
  \leq \mathcal Q(\xi_K)
  \leq \mathcal Q(\xi_G)=M.
```

Thus `A(K) = M`. Equality in the quadratic comparison makes all the Mamikon
convexity gaps vanish. The existing tangent equations and cap-kernel argument
then give `h_K(t) - h_G(t) = a cos(t)` on `[0, pi]`. Therefore

```math
K=K_G+(a,0),\qquad K\setminus\mathcal N(K)=G+(a,0).
```

Together with existence of a right-angle maximizer, the value statement bounds
`A(C)` by `M` for **every** right-angle cap `C`. It also bounds every sofa with a
right-angle motion by comparison with a maximizing monotone sofa. The reverse
direction of the cap classification now follows from horizontal-translation
invariance. It does not need the global bound at other angles.

The implication "every maximizer is Gerver" would be vacuous without existence;
keep the existence input visible in this passage.

### C. Recover the global bound

For a moving sofa `S` with `|S| >= 11/5`, obtain an admissible angle `omega` and
choose a fixed-angle maximizing monotone sofa `T`. Then `|T| >= |S| >= 11/5`.
The pinned-bound argument is valid for this maximizer with that threshold; it
does not require `|T| = M`. It gives a right-angle motion of
`R_(pi/2-omega) T`, so

```math
|S|\leq |T|=|R_{\pi/2-\omega}T|\leq M.
```

If `|S| < 11/5`, the bound follows from `M > 11/5`. Gerver itself is feasible,
so it is globally optimal. Only now derive `A_omega(C) <= M` for all caps and
all angles from fixed-angle existence and comparison.

The selected `T` above is a **numerical comparator**. There is no assertion
that it contains a rigid image of the arbitrary input `S`.

### D. Recover every equality-case sofa

Now take a given sofa `S` with `|S| = M`. Its own monotone envelopes contain the
relevant rigid images of `S`, and their areas equal `M` by the bound just proved.
Their caps are genuinely maximizing. Use the same maximizer-level right-angle
motion and cap classification, preserving the containment chain. Finally use
closedness of `S` and regular closedness of Gerver's sofa to obtain equality of
sets. Do not replace this chain by a comparison with an unrelated selected
maximizer, or infer set equality from equality of areas alone.

This is the role of the existing recovery section; it is not eliminated by
reorganizing optimality.

## 5. Where to put the discussion in this paper

The least disruptive integration is a **subsection at the end of Section 8**,
"Optimality from the maximizer principles", after the Mamikon rigidity
analysis, with a precise theorem and proof of Steps B and C. Introduce the
maximizer-level statements earlier, and postpone the equality-case application
of Section 3 until that subsection has supplied the bound. Section 3 can stay
as a roadmap, but its logical forward references must be explicit.

For a cleaner full reorganization, use this order:

- Introduction and primitive background inputs.
- Definition of a maximizing cap and fixed-angle existence/comparison.
- Penalized selection, variations, pinned bounds, and right-angle motion.
- Curvature and injectivity for an arbitrary right-angle maximizer.
- Equality in `Q`, classification of right-angle maximizing caps, and the
  corollary deriving global optimality.
- The given sofa's monotone-envelope reduction and regular-closed recovery.
- Formalization, provenance, and open questions.
