# A genuine sharp hand-area theorem from two independently filled triangular cores

**Theorem TCG1 (an exact positive answer on a nontrivial filled-core class).**
For every
\[
\boxed{\frac{11}{10}\le T\le\frac65,\qquad
\frac25\le a,b\le\frac45,}
\tag{TCG.1}
\]
take **independent** convex triangular cores
\[
V_a=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(T,0),(a,\tfrac12)\},
\quad
V_b=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(T,0),(b,\tfrac12)\}
\]
and add the *same explicit central rectangle*
\(R_T=[0,T]\times[0,\tfrac12]\):
\[
U_a=V_a+R_T,\qquad U_b=V_b+R_T.
\]
Let \(N(U)\) denote the full positive-height
forbidden one-turn niche generated by the **complete**
conventional quarter-turn support family of U, and set
\(A_U=U\setminus N(U)\). Form the two-handed envelope
\[
E_{a,b,T}=A_{U_a}\cap\rho A_{U_b},
\qquad\rho(x,y)=(x,1-y).
\]

Then the **ordinary geometric area**, without any
assumption of symmetry between a and b, satisfies
\[
\boxed{
|E_{a,b,T}|
\le\frac{3T}{2}
-2\left(T+\frac12-\sqrt2\right)^2
\le\frac95-
2\left(\frac{17}{10}-\sqrt2\right)^2
<\frac{41}{25}<M.
}
\tag{TCG.2}
\]

Here \(M=1+4Y^2+\arctan Y\) with
\(4Y^3+3Y-1=0,\ Y>0\) is Romik's exact
feasible candidate area.

This is a **strict proof at the exact sharp target**
for a two-parameter infinite family of distinct
rectangular-core combinations, not another
computer-assisted distant upper bound.
It uses only hand geometry of convex Minkowski sums,
ordinary area of two \(45^\circ\) inner forbidden
triangles and elementary rational comparisons.
It does **not** assert that all sofas have these
triangular cores, and therefore it does not
settle unrestricted ambidextrous optimality.

## 1. The filling rectangle contributes an exact, shape-independent outer area

Each triangle \(V_a\) has
\(|V_a|=T/4\), width T and height 1/2.
Minkowski addition by a horizontal segment
of length T adds exactly
\(T \times(\text{vertical span})=T/2\)
to its area. Subsequent addition of the
vertical segment of length 1/2 adds
\((1/2)\times(\text{new horizontal span})=T\).
Hence
\[
\boxed{|U_a|=|U_b|
=\frac T4+\frac T2+T=\frac{7T}4.}
\tag{TCG.3}
\]

Both caps are convex, downward closed,
lie in \([0,2T]\times[0,1]\), and contain
the **whole half-height rectangle**
\([0,2T]\times[0,1/2]\).
Their top horizontal faces are respectively
\[
\boxed{J_a=[a,a+T],\quad J_b=[b,b+T].}
\tag{TCG.4}
\]

Write \(A_a(x),A_b(x)\) for their outer
upper roofs. Thus \(A_a,A_b\ge1/2\).
Every vertical fiber of the *outer*
two-cap intersection \(K=U_a\cap\rho U_b\)
is the nonempty interval
\[
[1-A_b(x),\,A_a(x)]
\quad(0\le x\le2T).
\]
Integrating its actual nonnegative length gives
\[
\boxed{
|K|=|U_a|+|U_b|-2T
=\frac{3T}{2}.
}
\tag{TCG.5}
\]

This is the exact “two curved cores plus
filled center” area accounting. It uses
no turning-area functional or weighted
optimality theorem.

## 2. The two independent turning constraints each cut out a genuine triangle

At angle \(t=\pi/4\), put
\(c=s=1/\sqrt2\),
\(u=(c,s)\), \(v=(-s,c)\).
Directly maximize the support functions
over the three triangle vertices and
the rectangular summand:
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_a&=h_{U_a}(u)
=\frac{T+1/2+\max(T,a+1/2)}{\sqrt2},\\
g_a&=h_{U_a}(v)
=\frac{1/2+(1/2-a)_+}{\sqrt2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{TCG.6}
\]

The lower-turn *open inner forbidden quadrant*
at this real visited angle has two
wall roofs with slopes +1 and -1.
Its positive-height region is precisely
an isosceles open triangle \(\mathcal N_a\)
with base
\[
\boxed{
L_a=\sqrt2-\frac12-(\tfrac12-a)_+,\qquad
R_a=T+\frac12+\max(T,a+\tfrac12)-\sqrt2,
}
\tag{TCG.7}
\]
horizontal apex abscissa \((L_a+R_a)/2\)
and apex height
\[
\boxed{
h_a=\frac{R_a-L_a}{2}
\ge T+\frac12-\sqrt2>0.
}
\tag{TCG.8}
\]
Its exact ordinary area is \(h_a^2\).

Both base edges lie **inside the intersection of
the two different top-face intervals**,
uniformly throughout TCG.1:
\[
\boxed{
\frac45<L_a<R_a<T+\frac25,
\qquad
[\tfrac45,T+\tfrac25]
\subseteq J_a\cap J_b.
}
\tag{TCG.9}
\]

To check the two ends without approximations,
use \(7/5<\sqrt2<3/2\).
If \(a\le1/2\), then
\(L_a=\sqrt2-1+a>\frac75-1+\frac25=\frac45\);
if \(a\ge1/2\), then
\(L_a=\sqrt2-\frac12>\frac9{10}>\frac45\).
If \(a\ge T-1/2\), then
\(R_a=T+a+1-\sqrt2
<T+\frac45+1-\frac75=T+\frac25\).
If \(a\le T-1/2\), then
\(R_a=2T+1/2-\sqrt2
<2(\frac65)+\frac12-\frac75=\frac32
\le T+\frac25\)
because \(T\ge11/10\).
Finally, \(J_a\) and \(J_b\) both
contain \([\frac45,T+\frac25]\)
since \(a,b\le4/5\) and \(a,b\ge2/5\).
This proves TCG.9. In addition,
\[
h_a=\frac{R_a-L_a}{2}
<\frac{T+2/5-4/5}{2}
\le\frac25<\frac12.
\tag{TCG.10}
\]

The entire lower triangle \(\mathcal N_a\)
therefore lies inside **both outer caps**:
its y-coordinate lies in [0,2/5],
where U_a contains the full half-height
rectangle, and its x-coordinate lies in
J_b, where \(\rho U_b\) contains
the full height interval [0,1].

The reflected upper-turn forbidden triangle
\(\rho\mathcal N_b\), generated from U_b
at the reflected \(45^\circ\) frame,
lies in heights [3/5,1]; it is
inside \(\rho U_b\) by its own bottom
half-height rectangle and inside U_a
because its x-coordinate lies in J_a.
These two triangles are **disjoint**,
and each is truly forbidden by one of the
required canonical full quarter-turn
families. Consequently
\[
\boxed{
|E_{a,b,T}|
\le |K|-|\mathcal N_a|-|\rho\mathcal N_b|
\le\frac{3T}{2}
-2(T+\tfrac12-\sqrt2)^2.
}
\tag{TCG.11}
\]

We did not claim these two triangles
account for the *entire* continuous niche.
Additional forbidden positions can
only decrease the true envelope area,
which is why this short two-frame
comparison has the correct upper-bound
direction.

## 3. The exact constant comparison is a one-line square

Put
\(F(T)=\frac32T-2(T+\frac12-\sqrt2)^2\).
Then
\[
F'(T)=4\sqrt2-4T-\frac12
>4(\tfrac75)-4(\tfrac65)-\tfrac12
=\frac3{10}>0
\]
on the whole interval
\(11/10\le T\le6/5\).
So \(F(T)\le F(6/5)\).

The **rational** comparison
\(17^2=289>288=2\cdot12^2\)
gives \(\sqrt2<17/12\), and hence
\[
\frac{17}{10}-\sqrt2
>\frac{\sqrt2}{5}>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
F(6/5)
=\frac95-2(\frac{17}{10}-\sqrt2)^2
<\frac95-\frac4{25}=\frac{41}{25}.
\]

The existing exact candidate constant formula
gives \(M>41/25\). For completeness,
the monotone cubic's root satisfies
\(Y>297/1000\) and
\(\arctan Y\ge Y-Y^3/3\), which yields
\(M>41/25\) by elementary rational
substitution. This finishes TCG1.

## 4. What this verifies about the expanded-Gerver idea

This is a concrete area inequality in the
**intended core-plus-rectangle variables**.
It permits independent asymmetric parameters
\(a\ne b\) and is valid for the entire
real parameter rectangle TCG.1.
Neither independent Gerver optimality,
smooth-contact variation nor any global
curvature bound enters the proof.

The mechanism is visible:
- filling the two half-height cores creates
  a central rectangle and a fixed outer
  overlap area \(3T/2\);
- one real lower and one real upper hallway
  remove two disjoint, explicitly computed
  triangular regions;
- that exact lost area more than offsets
  the tempting “free center” gain.

**The limitation is essential.**
The genuine Romik core has a curved outer
boundary, not an arbitrary triangle.
For smooth near-optimal cores, a single
\(45^\circ\) niche triangle does **not**
capture the entire sharp niche cost;
a proof would have to integrate their
continuously moving inner-wall boundaries
and handle arbitrary mismatched top-face
and projection intervals. TCG1 supplies
a verified proof-of-concept for the
rectangular-core method, **not** the
unrestricted sharp-optimality theorem.

No Lean formalization, large optimizer,
CI or numerical area certificate was used.
